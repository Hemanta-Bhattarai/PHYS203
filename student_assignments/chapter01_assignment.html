Chapter 1 Assignment: Units and Dimensions

PHYS203 General Physics I · 10 problems (6 Homework-Hard + 4 Synthesis) · Days 2–3

Part A — Homework Hard Problems

HW-HARD Q1Day 2 A tank holds 600 gallons of water. Convert this volume to cubic meters. (Give your answer in cubic meters.)
HW-HARD Q2Day 2

(a) A plane flies at 500 mph. Convert to km/h. (Give your answer in km/h.)

(b) A plane flies at 500 mph. Convert to m/s. (Give your answer in m/s.)

HW-HARD Q3Day 2 A wire is 300 feet long. Convert to meters. (Give your answer in meters.)
HW-HARD Q4Day 3 True or False: (1/2)I*omega^2 has the same dimensions as (1/2)mv^2.
HW-HARD Q5Day 3 Find the dimensions required for G in F = G*m1*m2/r^2.
HW-HARD Q6Day 3 Find the dimensions of torque.

Part B — Synthesis Problems

SYNTHESIS Q1Day 2

A pharmaceutical manufacturer ships a drug in vials labeled 250 mg/mL. A hospital pharmacist needs to prepare an IV drip delivering 15 mg per minute to a patient, using a pump that dispenses in mL/hour. Find the required pump rate in mL/hour, showing every conversion factor used explicitly.

SYNTHESIS Q2Day 2

A shipping company charges by "dimensional weight," computed as (length × width × height in inches) / 139, in pounds. A box measuring 40 cm × 30 cm × 25 cm has an actual mass of 8 kg. Find the dimensional weight in pounds, convert the actual mass to pounds, and determine which value the shipping company will charge for (whichever is larger).

SYNTHESIS Q3Day 3

An online forum post proposes that the period of a swinging pendulum is given by T = 2π√(m/g) (using mass instead of length). Evaluate this formula using dimensional analysis, identify precisely what is wrong, and propose what physical quantity would need to replace m to fix the dimensional inconsistency.

SYNTHESIS Q4Day 3

A student encounters the proposed formula F = (kqQ)/(r) for the electric force between two charges (Coulomb's law), where k has dimensions [M][L]³[T]-⁴[I]-² and q,Q have dimensions of electric charge [I][T]. Evaluate whether this formula (with r, not r², in the denominator) can be dimensionally correct for a force, or whether it must be rejected.
