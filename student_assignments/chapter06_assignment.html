Chapter 6 Assignment: Momentum and Collisions

PHYS203 General Physics I · 10 problems (6 Homework-Hard + 4 Synthesis) · Days 17–18

Part A — Homework Hard Problems

HW-HARD Q1Day 17 A 1250 kg car traveling at 20 m/s collides head-on with a stationary 950 kg car, locking together. Find the combined velocity. (Give your answer in m/s.)
HW-HARD Q2Day 17 A 1650 kg car moving at 18 m/s and a 1150 kg car moving at 14 m/s in the same direction collide and move together. Find their combined velocity. (Give your answer in m/s.)
HW-HARD Q3Day 17 Two objects (4.5 kg at 5.5 m/s and 6.5 kg at -3.5 m/s) collide and stick together. Find the combined velocity (positive = direction of the 4.5 kg object). (Give your answer in m/s.)
HW-HARD Q4Day 18

(a) Two carts (2.5 kg and 6.5 kg) undergo an elastic collision; the 2.5 kg cart moves at 11 m/s toward the stationary 6.5 kg cart. Find the 2.5 kg cart's final velocity. (Give your answer in m/s.)

(b) Same collision: find the 6.5 kg cart's final velocity. (Give your answer in m/s.)

HW-HARD Q5Day 18

(a) Three identical 2.5 kg carts are in a line; the first (5.5 m/s) strikes the second, sticking, then strikes the third, sticking. Find the velocity after the first sub-collision. (Give your answer in m/s.)

(b) Same sequence: find the final velocity of all three carts combined. (Give your answer in m/s.)

HW-HARD Q6Day 18

(a) A perfectly inelastic collision between a 5.5 kg object (7.5 m/s) and a stationary 16 kg object. Find the combined velocity. (Give your answer in m/s.)

(b) Same collision: find the fraction of kinetic energy lost.

Part B — Synthesis Problems

SYNTHESIS Q1Day 17

A 1300 kg car traveling at 22 m/s crashes into a rigid barrier and comes to rest in 0.15 s due to crumple-zone deformation. Find the average force on the car, then find what that force would have been if the crumple zone instead extended the stopping time to 0.4 s, and discuss the safety implication using Newton's second law and impulse.

SYNTHESIS Q2Day 17

A 1450 kg car traveling at 25 m/s is brought to rest by a combination of braking (providing a known constant force of 6000 N) and a final 0.2 s crumple-zone impact with a barrier. Find the time spent braking before impact (assuming braking alone would reduce speed to 8 m/s right before the crumple-zone impact), then find the average force from the crumple zone during the final impact phase.

SYNTHESIS Q3Day 18

A 1400 kg car traveling at 18 m/s rear-ends a stationary 1100 kg car, and the two lock together (perfectly inelastic). Find their combined velocity, the kinetic energy lost in the collision, and (given that crumple zones convert this lost energy into structural deformation over an effective 0.3 m of crush distance) the average force involved in that deformation.

SYNTHESIS Q4Day 18

A 1550 kg car traveling at 20 m/s rear-ends a stationary 1050 kg car, locking together. Find their combined velocity, the kinetic energy lost, and (if this energy converts entirely into crumpling over 0.35 m) the average crumple-zone force involved.
