Kinematic Equations

v = v₀ + at
v² = v₀² + 2a(x − x₀)
x = x₀ + ½(v + v₀)t
x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²

Preliminaries

  • See that you have a motion detector (green or blue) plugged into a Vernier LabPro interface, and this in turn connected via a USB cable to the computer.
  • Start up Logger Pro: (Start / All Programs / Vernier / Logger Pro)
  • If the motion detector and LabPro are properly attached, Logger Pro will automatically set up graphs for velocity and position.
  • Aim the sensor across the table, click the collect button, and move something toward and away from it. Note that it only senses in a cone out in front, it senses the closest object that reflects ultrasound, and it doesn't work closer than about 40 cm for the blue sensors and 15 cm for the green ones.
  • On the Moodle page for PHYS 203 General Physics (not the PHYS 203 Lab page), under Lab Materials, find the folder Lab 2 Files for Logger Pro. Click on it and then click to download the folder. It will be a compressed folder. Find it in your Downloads folder, right-click on it, and extract all files. You can then delete the compressed folder.

A Little Fun

In Logger Pro, go to File | Open, then navigate to your Downloads folder, where you should see all the files you just downloaded. Each file sets up a graph showing a plot line that you try to reproduce by moving the object. For each file below, get a good result and show your instructor — be sure both partners (or all three) succeed on each one.

Position Matching

  • GC Position Match 1 Green or Blue
  • GC Position Match 2 Green or Blue
  • GC Position Match Custom *

* While one partner looks away, the other records a run with interesting motions (Experiment | Store Latest Run). The partner who looked away then tries to reproduce it.

Discussion — GC Position Match 1 and 2

Before moving the car, discuss and write down — on paper — how the car needs to move (direction, speeding up or slowing down, stopping) to produce the plotted position graph. Then try it. Does your prediction work?

Velocity Matching

  • GC Velocity Match 1
  • GC Velocity Match 2
  • GC Velocity Match Custom *

* Same custom-run procedure as above, this time matching a velocity graph.

Discussion — GC Velocity Match 1 and 2
  1. Before moving the car, discuss and write down how it needs to move, and sketch the x-t (position-time) graph you'd expect this velocity motion to produce.
  2. Move the car and compare: did your predicted x-t graph match what Logger Pro actually shows?

Freefall

Open the file GC zBall Toss.cmbl. Set the sensor on the table or the floor, aiming straight up.

Toss a ball straight up over the sensor, pulling your hands back immediately, until you get a plot with a nice long parabolic section. (Alternative: hold the sensor up above and toss a ball underneath it, keeping the sensor very still.)

Look at the data. Only part of it is freefall — find that portion before continuing.

  1. 1Click and drag to highlight the freefall portion of the acceleration plot, then use Analyze | Statistics.
Discussion 1

a. Does the mean have any meaning, and why is it close to 9.8 m/s²?

  1. 2Highlight the freefall portion of the velocity plot, then use Analyze | Linear Fit.
Discussion 2
  1. What are the units (dimensions) of parameters m and b?
  2. What physical quantities do m and b relate to? Do you agree with the values of m and b?
  1. 3Highlight the freefall portion of the distance plot, then use Analyze | Curve Fit and choose a quadratic.
Discussion 3
  1. What are the units of parameters A, B, and C?
  2. What physical quantities do A, B, and C mean? Do you agree with the values of A, B, and C? (For example, if B = 23, what physical thing is 23, and what are its units?)
  1. 4Once you've interpreted the coefficients, check the box called "time offset" — this shifts the fit's time axis to start at the beginning of the highlighted region.
Discussion 4

After checking "time offset," do you agree with the new values of A, B, and C? What did the time offset do to the analysis, and why does the value make more sense now?
