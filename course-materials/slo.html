Learning Outcomes

Course-level, chapter-level, and day-by-day Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs).

SLO: PHYS203 General Physics I

Course Overview

A calculus and vector-based treatment of mechanics, oscillations, waves, and sound. Students build a single connected toolkit: precise description of motion (kinematics, vectors), prediction of motion (Newton’s laws and applications), global accounting of motion (momentum, energy, rotation), and extension of that reasoning to oscillating and wave systems.

Chapter wise Schedule

Chapter 1: Units and Dimensions

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Identify and convert between base and derived SI units.
  • Apply dimensional analysis to check the validity of a physical equation.

Day 1 (Class Day 2): Units

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Identify the SI base units for length, mass, and time, and convert between unit systems (e.g., metric/imperial).
  • Use unit conversion factors to solve multi-step conversion problems.

Day 2 (Class Day 3): Dimensions

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Determine the dimensions of a physical quantity from its defining equation.
  • Apply dimensional analysis to verify whether an equation is dimensionally consistent.

Chapter 2: One-Dimensional Kinematics

Topics

  • Position, velocity, and acceleration (average and instantaneous)
  • Graphical analysis and the connection to calculus
  • Kinematic equations and free fall

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define, calculate, and interpret average and instantaneous velocity and acceleration, connecting each to slope and to calculus.
  • Apply kinematic equations to constant-acceleration and free-fall problems.

Day 1 (Class Day 4): Position, Velocity, and Acceleration (Average); Graphical Analysis

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Calculate average velocity and average acceleration from position or velocity data.
  • Interpret a position-time graph, connecting average velocity to the slope of a secant line.

Day 2 (Class Day 5): Instantaneous Velocity/Acceleration; Connecting Graphs to Calculus

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define instantaneous velocity and acceleration as derivatives of position and velocity, connecting each to the slope of a tangent line.
  • Interpret and analyze position, velocity, and acceleration graphs together as a connected set.

Day 3 (Class Day 6): Kinematic Equations for Constant Acceleration

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Select and apply the correct kinematic equation to solve a one-dimensional constant-acceleration problem.

Day 4 (Class Day 7): Free Fall

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Apply kinematic equations to analyze free-fall motion under gravity, including objects thrown upward or dropped from rest.

Chapter 3: Vectors

Topics

  • Vector representation, components, addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication
  • Vector decomposition

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Represent, add, subtract, and scale vectors both graphically and algebraically.
  • Decompose vectors into components for use in force and motion analysis.

Day 1 (Class Day 8): Vector Representation, Components, Addition, Subtraction, Scalar Multiplication

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Represent a vector graphically and algebraically, calculate its components, and reconstruct magnitude/direction from components.
  • Add, subtract, and scale vectors both graphically (head-to-tail) and algebraically (by components).

Day 2 (Class Day 9): Vector Decomposition

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Decompose a vector into components along a specified pair of perpendicular axes, including tilted (incline) axes.
  • Apply vector decomposition to analyze forces and motion in two dimensions.

Chapter 4: Two-Dimensional Motion

Topics

  • Simple 2D motion (extending kinematics to two dimensions)
  • Projectile motion
  • Uniform circular motion

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Extend one-dimensional kinematics to two-dimensional motion using vector components.
  • Analyze projectile motion using kinematic equations and vector decomposition.
  • Analyze uniform circular motion and calculate centripetal acceleration and force.

Day 1 (Class Day 10): Simple 2D Motion

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Represent position, velocity, and acceleration in two dimensions as vectors with independent x- and y-components.
  • Recognize that horizontal and vertical motion can be analyzed independently.

Day 2 (Class Day 11): Projectile Motion

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Decompose projectile motion into independent horizontal and vertical components and set up the governing equations.
  • Calculate range, maximum height, and time of flight for a projectile.

Day 3 (Class Day 12): Uniform Circular Motion

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Describe uniform circular motion and identify the direction of velocity and centripetal acceleration.
  • Calculate centripetal acceleration and centripetal force for an object in uniform circular motion.

Chapter 5: Newton’s Laws and Applications

Topics

  • Newton’s three laws and common forces
  • F = ma in multiple contexts
  • Inclined planes
  • Frictional forces

Student Learning Outcomes

  • State and apply Newton’s first, second, and third laws, identifying common forces (weight, normal, tension).
  • Apply F = ma to systems involving multiple forces, including inclined planes and friction.

Day 1 (Class Day 13): Newton’s Laws and Different Forces

Micro student learning outcomes

  • State and apply Newton’s first, second, and third laws of motion.
  • Identify and calculate weight, normal force, and tension in common scenarios.

Day 2 (Class Day 14): F = ma in Multiple Contexts

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Apply Newton’s second law to systems involving multiple forces, such as connected objects.

Day 3 (Class Day 15): Inclined Planes

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Analyze motion on inclined planes using Newton’s laws and vector decomposition.
  • Calculate acceleration and normal force for objects on inclined planes.

Day 4 (Class Day 16): Frictional Forces

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Distinguish static from kinetic friction and calculate frictional force using f = μN.
  • Determine whether an object remains stationary or begins to slide, given a coefficient of friction.

Chapter 6: Momentum and Collisions

Topics

  • Momentum, impulse, and conservation of momentum
  • Elastic and inelastic collisions

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define momentum and impulse, and apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions.
  • Distinguish elastic from inelastic collisions and calculate kinetic energy loss in inelastic collisions.

Day 1 (Class Day 17): Momentum, Impulse, and Conservation of Momentum

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define linear momentum and impulse, and apply the impulse-momentum theorem.
  • Apply conservation of momentum to collisions and explosions.

Day 2 (Class Day 18): Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Distinguish elastic from inelastic collisions and analyze each using conservation of momentum.
  • Calculate the loss of kinetic energy in an inelastic collision.

Chapter 7: Work and Energy

Topics

  • Work and the work-energy theorem
  • Potential energy and conservation of energy
  • Applications of conservation of energy

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define and calculate work, and apply the work-energy theorem.
  • Define gravitational and elastic potential energy, and apply conservation of mechanical energy to multi-step systems.

Day 1 (Class Day 19): Work and the Work-Energy Theorem

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define work done by constant and variable forces.
  • State and apply the work-energy theorem to relate work and changes in kinetic energy.

Day 2 (Class Day 20): Potential Energy and Conservation of Energy

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define gravitational and elastic potential energy and calculate changes in each.
  • State and apply conservation of mechanical energy for systems with only conservative forces.

Day 3 (Class Day 21): Applications of Conservation of Energy

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Apply conservation of mechanical energy to multi-step and real-world systems (ramps, pendulums, springs combined with height changes).
  • Account for energy lost to non-conservative forces (e.g., friction) within the conservation framework.

Chapter 8: Rotational Motion

Topics

  • Angular kinematics
  • Rotational kinetic energy, moment of inertia, and conservation of energy in rotation
  • Torque and Newton’s second law for rotation
  • Angular momentum
  • Rolling and gyroscopic motion

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define and relate angular position, velocity, and acceleration to their linear counterparts.
  • Calculate rotational kinetic energy and moment of inertia, and apply torque, Newton’s second law for rotation, and conservation of angular momentum.
  • Analyze rolling motion and describe gyroscopic behavior.

Day 1 (Class Day 22): Angular Kinematics

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define angular position, velocity, and acceleration, and relate them to linear quantities for circular motion.
  • Apply kinematic equations to rotational motion with constant angular acceleration.

Day 2 (Class Day 23): Rotational Kinetic Energy, Moment of Inertia, and Conservation of Energy in Rotational Motion

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define rotational kinetic energy and calculate moment of inertia for common shapes.
  • Apply conservation of energy to systems involving both rotational and translational kinetic energy.

Day 3 (Class Day 24): Torque I

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define torque as the rotational equivalent of force and calculate it using τ = rF sin θ.

Day 4 (Class Day 25): Torque II and Newton’s Second Law for Rotation

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Apply Newton’s second law for rotation (τ_net = Iα) to solve problems involving rotational motion.

Day 5 (Class Day 26): Angular Momentum and Its Conservation

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define angular momentum (L = Iω) as the rotational equivalent of linear momentum.
  • Apply conservation of angular momentum to solve problems involving changes in rotational motion.

Day 6 (Class Day 27): Rolling Motion and Gyroscopic Motion

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Analyze rolling motion as a combination of translational and rotational motion and calculate its total kinetic energy.
  • Describe gyroscopic motion and its effect on the stability of rotating objects.

Chapter 9: Oscillations

Topics

  • Simple harmonic motion

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define simple harmonic motion and analyze its key properties (amplitude, period, frequency, phase).
  • Apply SHM equations to mass-spring systems and pendulums.

Day 1 (Class Day 28): Simple Harmonic Motion I

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define simple harmonic motion as periodic motion with a restoring force proportional to displacement.
  • Identify and calculate amplitude, period, frequency, and phase for an SHM system.

Day 2 (Class Day 29): Simple Harmonic Motion II (Mass-Spring Systems and Pendulums)

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Apply Hooke’s law (F = −kx) and the mass-spring period formula, T = 2π√(m/k).
  • Apply the simple pendulum period formula, T = 2π√(L/g), under the small-angle approximation.

Chapter 10: Waves

Topics

  • Wave properties
  • Superposition, interference, and wave types
  • Traveling and standing waves

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Define and calculate wave properties, relating them using the wave equation.
  • Analyze superposition, interference, and distinguish transverse from longitudinal waves.
  • Analyze traveling and standing waves, including normal mode frequencies.

Day 1 (Class Day 30): Wave Properties

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define and calculate wavelength, frequency, speed, and amplitude for a wave.
  • Relate wave properties using the wave equation v = fλ.

Day 2 (Class Day 31): Superposition, Interference, and Wave Types

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define superposition and analyze constructive and destructive interference.
  • Distinguish transverse from longitudinal waves and identify real-world examples of each.

Day 3 (Class Day 32): Traveling and Standing Waves

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define traveling waves and standing waves, distinguishing their behavior.
  • Analyze the conditions for standing waves and calculate normal mode frequencies for strings.

Chapter 11: Sound

Topics

  • Production, propagation, and speed of sound
  • Sound intensity and decibels
  • The Doppler effect

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Describe how sound is produced and propagates through different media.
  • Calculate sound intensity and decibel levels.
  • Apply the Doppler effect formula to moving sources and observers.

Day 1 (Class Day 33): Production, Propagation, and Speed of Sound

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Describe how sound is produced by vibrating objects and propagates through solids, liquids, and gases.
  • Calculate the speed of sound in different media, accounting for temperature.

Day 2 (Class Day 34): Sound Intensity and Decibels

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Define sound intensity and calculate it using I = P/A.
  • Define decibels and calculate decibel levels using dB = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀).

Day 3 (Class Day 35): The Doppler Effect

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Describe the Doppler effect as a change in observed frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.
  • Calculate observed frequency for moving source, moving observer, and both moving scenarios.

Closing Sessions

Class Day 36: Semester Review

Class Day 37: Presentations

Micro student learning outcomes

  • Communicate physics reasoning and calculations clearly to peers by connecting a course topic to a real-world system (SLO 6).
