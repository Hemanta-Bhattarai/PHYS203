Course Description

A calculus and vector treatment of basic physics. This is the first semester, which includes mechanics of motion, vibration and sound. Required of physics majors. Offered every fall.

Prerequisite or Concurrent Course

Math 211

Course Learning Outcomes

At the end of this course, students will be able to:

  • Analyze vectors and apply them in multi-dimensional kinematics and dynamics.
  • Use calculus (differentiation and integration) to model and solve mechanics problems.
  • Apply Newton's laws and mechanics principles to describe and predict motion.
  • Solve problems involving energy, momentum, rotational motion, oscillations, and waves.
  • Interpret and evaluate experimental data, including uncertainty analysis.
  • Communicate solutions clearly using equations, diagrams, and scientific reasoning.

Detailed day-by-day learning outcomes for all 34 class sessions are available in the companion course website under Learning Outcomes.

Test Coverage and Tentative Dates

Tests are held during the Tuesday lab period, on the first Tuesday on or after the date the relevant chapter concludes. Dates below are tentative and may shift slightly as the semester proceeds; confirmed dates will be posted in Moodle.

  • Test 1 (tentative: Tuesday, Oct 6) covers units and dimensions, one-dimensional motion, vectors, and two-dimensional motion.
  • Test 2 (tentative: Tuesday, Nov 3) covers Newton's laws, momentum and collisions, and work and energy, the material between Test 1 and Test 3.
  • Test 3 (tentative: Tuesday, Nov 17) covers material up to and including rotational motion.
  • Final Exam (Dec 8–10) is comprehensive, covering the full semester.

Academic Integrity

Course assignments and exams are important ways to demonstrate your knowledge of the course content; therefore they should represent your best, authentic work. Academic dishonesty is any act that misrepresents your academic work or interferes with the academic work of others. Examples include, but are not limited to, the following: cheating, falsification of data, submission of the same assignment in more than one class, sabotaging the work of another, or plagiarism.

Plagiarism is the use of someone else's ideas or words (sentences, clauses, or distinct phrases) without quotation or citation.

Generative AI is a type of artificial intelligence that creates new content, such as images, text, and audio, based on patterns in existing data. The use of generative AI to complete assignments is considered an academic integrity violation when it misrepresents content as students' original work and/or does not adhere to a professor's stated course policy. In this course, the use of generative AI is PROHIBITED, meaning that students are not permitted to use generative AI tools when completing any type of assignment that will be submitted for assessment/grading.

Cases of academic dishonesty are reported to the Associate Academic Dean, who oversees the academic integrity policy. Penalties for academic dishonesty are listed in the Goshen College catalog and range from redoing the assignment to dismissal from the college.
